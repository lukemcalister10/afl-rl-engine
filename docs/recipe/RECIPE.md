# THE RECIPE — how the redesigned model values players

plain-English specification · derived from the running code and adversarially verified · 2026-08-05

**Scope.** This recipe describes the redesign — the owner's word for the model specified here. One honest sentence first: the board shipping today still prices from the superseded pick curve until the redesign lands; the steps below that state the redesign's ruled method say so explicitly where the shipped artifact was derived otherwise (List 2 step 32; List 3 steps 30–31).

**Structure.** Three lists: List 1 — the store (every ingredient the engine reads); List 2 — prediction (player history → expected future output); List 3 — valuation (expected output → the number on the board).

**Units.** Every quantity names its units: scoring levels are in points per game (season-average scoring rate); money is in board value points, the unit in which national draft pick 1 = 3,000; counts, years, and fractions are labelled where they appear.

---

## LIST 1 — THE STORE (ingredients inventory)

### A. What the store is

1. One flat list of 2,651 player records — every player who entered the league by any route from 2003 through 2026, active and retired alike. There is exactly one authored copy; a checksum of the whole file is pinned in a boot manifest and asserted before any engine run (the pin's first 8 hex digits are 81d24704). Every derived artifact is stamped with the checksum of the store it was built from.

### B. Per-player fields the engine reads

2. Full name — text; used for identity tripwires, name matching in the weekly score feed, and display.
3. Name slug — lowercase hyphenated text (e.g. "joshua-kelly"); the join key that links a record to the injury register, the display-override list, and the published board.
4. Durable player ID — opaque text token; present only on the 804 currently-listed players; the weekly score-entry resolver attaches scores by this ID, and data-error reports key on it.
5. Entry year — calendar year (2003–2026) the player was drafted or signed.
6. Selection number — integer 1–80, or empty on the 283 players who entered without a selection; his overall pick in the draft he entered through. The numbering is the database's own continuous ordering per year (it can differ from the official published pick number).
7. Entry route — one of 9 codes (section F below); drives on-curve-vs-pool pricing, debut-year timing, and training-cohort membership.
8. Position at draft — one of 6 position codes (section F); the axis on which the cohort/pedigree curves are built.
9. Current position — one of 6 codes; the current-year leg of the player's own valuation.
10. Settled future position — one of 6 codes; the future-years leg — it selects his age curve, peak, and career runway. 341 records carry a future position different from the current one (340 of them retired players, plus one active player), so the two legs must not be assumed equal.
11. Current eligibility declaration — comma-separated list of 1–2 position codes; carried only on the 804 currently-listed players; sets the replacement bar for the season in progress. A tall code absorbs its matching small code (tall forward absorbs small forward, tall defender absorbs small defender); four tall/ruck-crossed-with-opposite-small combinations are treated as data errors and the row falls back to single-position.
12. Alternate future position — one of 6 codes, or empty; one allowed secondary future stream (87 players carry one).
13. Alternate-stream probability — percent, 0–100; the chance the player ends up in the alternate stream; that fraction of his future-years value is measured against the more favourable (lower) of the two replacement bars.
14. Career games total — count of league matches; used as the sample-size trust weight (games ÷ 130, clamped) when rolling a player's demonstrated form along the age curve. Owner-ruled at this audit: this becomes a derived sum of the season rows' games (the stored standalone counter is retired — it is stale on 569 records today; findings (g-1)).
15. Season scoring history — a list with one row per season played (11,264 rows across the store). Each row carries: the season year; the average fantasy points per game scored that season; the games played that season (count); and the position label for that season (a single code, or two codes joined by a slash for a season played across two roles). This is the engine's entire performance signal: per-season averages only, no per-match detail. Closed seasons take their own row's position for the fit bar; the live season takes the eligibility declaration instead.
16. Birth year — calendar year; the age clock. 302 records carry an explicit blank; the engine then assumes the player was 18 in his entry year.
17. Birth date — exact date, on 848 records; read only by the offline peak-model builder to grade how precisely a player's age is known — the live valuation never reads it.
18. Retired flag — yes/no; the 1,847 retired players are excluded from the current board and priced only on historical (as-of past year) boards.
19. Last year listed — calendar year, present on 13 records; the last season the player was on a club list. A value before the current season marks him delisted-not-retired: off the current board, recalled onto back-year boards.
20. Force-active flag — present on 63 records spanning entry years 2015 through 2026; keeps a player on the current board regardless of the other activity tests. 19 of the flagged rows do carry scoring rows with played games (current-season debutants), so the flag is not only for not-yet-debuted draftees with no scoring rows; only 47 of the 63 actually need the flag to pass the active filter.
21. Has-current-season flag — yes/no; a precomputed marker that the player has a scoring row for the season in progress; one input to the active-player filter.
22. No-selection flag — yes/no; true for the 283 players who entered with no draft selection (all six post-draft/supplemental routes).
23. Pick-curve exclusion flag — present on 3 records; those players are removed from the pool that teaches the pick-price curve (later same-year picks slide up one slot in curve attribution), but they are still valued and still appear on the board.
24. Draft concession category — free text (father-son, academy, next-generation academy, plus rare one-off strings); collapsed to four buckets for board analytics; not used in pricing.
25. Entry route long label — human-readable name of the entry route ("National", "Rookie", "Mid-Season", "Post-Draft – …"); display only.
26. Drafting club — text; the club that originally drafted/signed him; display and by-club analytics.
27. Current club — text, on currently-listed players; display, and a disambiguator when two active players share a name in the weekly score feed.
28. Fantasy-keeper-league owning team — text, on currently-listed players; which team in the owner's keeper league holds the player. Read only by the weekly movers report and the web-view join — never by the valuation.

### C. Present but unread

29. Five audit/provenance columns are carried on records but read by no live code anywhere: a simplified entry-stream code (which collapses five routes — the international, unregistered-pathway, and three post-draft-signing routes — into one "OTHER" bucket, while the mid-season and pre-season-supplemental routes keep their own codes), a within-stream selection number that is byte-for-byte identical to the main selection number on every row where either is present (fully redundant, not merely unread), a within-stream year, a free-text pick-correction note (on 1,041 rows), and a pick-source citation (on 8 rows).

### D. Global data the engine reads alongside the store

30. Season-state file — season year (2026), total rounds in the season (24), current round (20), calendar progress (fraction of season elapsed, 0–1; currently 0.83), and durable-player exposure pace (fraction, 0–1; currently 0.773). Prorates every games-played qualification bar for the in-progress season; advanced automatically by the weekly apply.
31. Last-national-pick table — for each of 23 draft years, the final national-draft selection number (integer). Its only live use is as a display offset: the median across all years offsets the pick scale of the debut projector for rookie-route players (the chaining machinery that once treated it as the authoritative offset for the store's continuous numbering has been deleted). After the store's own picks were renumbered continuous and gapless, the table disagrees with the store's own last national ordinal in 4 of the 23 years — it still records the pre-renumbering ends. A loud row-counting fallback covers any missing year.
32. Model parameter tables — for each of the 6 positions: peak score (fantasy points per game), peak age (years), and an empirical age curve (fraction of peak at each age, smoothed to decline at least 1% per year past peak).
33. Pass-mark tables — expected-score curves (fantasy points per game by career season 1–10) per pick band and per band-and-position; the 8 pick-band definitions with their anchor picks; and bust-rate priors per band.
34. Young-credit table — an expected-improvement credit grid indexed by selection number (about 90 pick columns), anchored at a 46-game experience basis; feeds the evidence-conditioned rerating credit for young players.
35. Bust prior table — probability-of-bust priors, one value per position per selection number 1–70.
36. Adopted pick-price curve — the board price (in board value points) of each national selection 1 through 64, with pick 1 = 3,000 points, plus a single pooled price for every entrant past 64; and a certified currency factor (1.0524) converting raw engine output into board points by division.
37. Injury/availability register — an owner-authored table, one row per injury window: player key, section (whether a return-season haircut applies), window ID, designation year, status, return year. The engine derives from it who is out for the remainder of the season, the lost fraction of his season (1 − games played ÷ 22), and whether a return haircut applies. Player rows join by name slug.
38. Injury-return table — an age-indexed surface storing the return-season haircut after a lost season (the fraction of prior output deducted, capped at 15%); expected retained output is one minus the stored haircut.
39. Owner display-override list — per-player-key multipliers applied to the displayed value only; the engine value is never touched.
40. Four frozen fitted models — binary snapshots, checksum-pinned, fitted only at a deliberate release "bake" and never at run time: a pick-quantile scoring model; a calibrated forward-projection tree ensemble; a rating surface for young/unplayed players keyed to a configuration signature; and a peak-projection model.
41. Governance pins — a boot manifest of checksums for the store, board, register and configuration, plus a ruled-configuration file and a release contract; any mismatch halts the run before the store is read.

### E. What feeds the store (one line each)

42. Weekly score entry — the owner pastes a per-round "name, score" list; each name is exact-matched (case/accents normalised, no fuzzy auto-attach) against the 804 active durable IDs; any non-match becomes a residue file for a one-tap owner confirm; a clean round writes a stamped snapshot. Writes the snapshot, not the store.
43. Weekly apply — takes that snapshot and merges each listed score into the player's current-season scoring row: running average updated, games count +1 (a listed 0 is a played game; absence from the file means did-not-play). Runs as a staged, validated, atomic transaction that also rebuilds the board, refreshes the season-state file, moves every checksum pin, and records the round in a ledger that blocks a double apply. The write gate ships OFF in this build (proofs run against scratch copies only).
44. Multi-round catch-up — the same weekly transaction applied sequentially, one round at a time, from several weekly files with one consolidated approval.
45. Post-commit finalize — regenerates derived outputs (movers report, web bundles) from the committed state; writes no store field.
46. Sibling repin — after a store advance, rebuilds the derived sibling boards/reference vectors and moves their pins; writes no store field.
47. Ownership apply — carries the owner's keeper-league CSV into the fantasy-owning-team field on store rows, moving all ten dependent checksum pins atomically; values do not move.
48. Injury register repin — after the owner hand-edits the injury register, validates every register key against the store and moves the register's checksum pin; never writes the store.
49. Draft results, new intakes, retirements and delistings — NO standing feed exists: new rows, the retired flag, last-listed year, positions and route codes are owner-curated hand edits, done through one-off dated migration scripts, after which the pins are re-stamped.

### F. Positions and entry routes — the actual value sets

50. Positions (6 codes; every position field uses exactly this set). Each carries its own replacement level (the engine's in-code constants, fantasy points per game), peak age, age curve, expected-score curves and bust priors:
   - MID — midfielder; replacement 80.1.
   - RUCK — ruck; replacement 78.5.
   - SD — small/general defender; replacement 78.3.
   - SF — small/general forward; replacement 70.9.
   - KPD — key-position (tall) defender; replacement 68.4.
   - KPF — key-position (tall) forward; replacement 66.8.
51. Entry routes (9 codes in the data; a 10th handled in code). Operationally the code distinguishes three things: on-curve vs pool pricing, debut-year timing, and training-cohort membership.
   - ND — national draft (1,569 rows): the only route priced on the pick curve, for selections 1–64; a national selection 65 or deeper joins the pool. National rows teach the pick curve.
   - RD — rookie draft (693): pool entrant — order of selection carries no value; still a member of the pedigree/training cohorts.
   - MSD — mid-season draft (106): pool; the one route whose players debut in their own entry year (all other routes debut the following year), so its age cycle shifts back one year; excluded from the calibration training pool.
   - SSP — pre-season supplemental signing (52): pool; no selection number.
   - IRE — international (Irish) recruit signing (57): pool; no selection number.
   - UNR — unregistered-pathway signing (59): a real recruiting route — a player not registered for the draft, signed afterwards; NOT missing data. Pool; no selection number.
   - PDA — post-draft academy signing (51): pool; no selection number.
   - PDN — post-draft next-generation-academy signing (43): pool; no selection number.
   - PDS — post-draft scholarship signing (21): pool; no selection number.
   - (Pre-season draft: a live handling branch exists — priced as pool — but zero rows currently carry the code; the 8 known pre-season draftees are filed under the rookie route with a source note.)

## LIST 2 — PREDICTION (player history → expected future output)

Scope note (read first): this engine never produces a standalone "expected per-season future output" number for a player. Its prediction object is a six-level band of plausible forward output (points per game), and every level of that band is immediately converted to money inside one loop; all later adjustments (bust risk, youth credit, staleness, sit-out) act on the money, not on the output levels. Steps 14 onward therefore describe expectation-shaping rules that are physically written in price space — the boundary with List 3 does not factor cleanly (see findings file, F2). Where a money unit is unavoidable it is called a "value unit"; the currency's definition belongs to List 3.

### A. Measuring a season

1. A player's record → keep, per season: the year, games played, the season's average fantasy points per game, and the position(s) recorded for that season; plus draft year, draft route, draft pick, birth year, current/future position declarations, and retirement/delist facts → the raw history.
2. A season's games and average → a season "qualifies" softly around a 10-game bar (a smooth ramp from ~0 at 9 games to ~1 at 13 games); the hard 6-, 14- and 22-game bars used elsewhere are scaled down by season progress for the season currently being played, but the hard count of 10-game qualifying seasons is deliberately NOT prorated (a season-prorated version was measured and rejected), one 12-game adequacy bar is likewise deliberately unprorated, and the key-forward sustained-level reading uses a separate 12-game bar that does prorate → per-season qualification weights in [0,1].
3. A season's average, for best-season and sustained-level readings only → multiplied by (long-run league mean ÷ that year's league mean of qualified season averages, 2009–2025) → an era-adjusted points-per-game level (the recency-weighted current level in step 7 is deliberately NOT era-adjusted).
4. Position declarations → each position carries a fixed replacement bar in points per game (mids highest ≈80, key forwards lowest ≈66.8), derived once from the pool of freely acquirable players and owner-adjusted; a season's bar is the eligibility declaration for that season, a dual declaration taking the lower (more favourable) bar → the bar all "output above replacement" is measured against.
5. Draft route → national-draft picks 1–64 sit on the pick ladder; every other entry route (rookie, pre-season, mid-season, overseas, post-draft signings, and national selections past 64) collapses to a single "pool" slot valued by position only, order of selection carrying no value → each player's effective pick (1–64 or pool).

### B. How much the record is trusted

6. The per-season qualification weights, summed over the career up to the as-of year → one evidence number E ("effective qualifying seasons") drives three smooth dials: trust-recent-form = E²/(E²+0.55²); treat-as-established = E³/(E³+3.6³); and a pedigree weight = (trust gate)×(0.11 + 0.89·e^(−E/1.1)) that fades toward a floor of 0.11 and never reaches zero → the weights that mix record against draft pedigree everywhere downstream.
7. Each season inside a level estimate → weighted by games²/(games+5.8) (thin seasons drowned) × a per-position-group recency decay per year of age of the season (≈0.23–0.40) → the recency-weighted current level, in points per game.

### C. The current demonstrated level (one number per player per as-of year)

8. A par-centred career level (the position/pick/tenure par plus the games-weighted level's excess over par, scaled by min(1, career exposure ÷ 22 games) — a thin record regresses toward the par of his position, pick and tenure, not toward a robust own-record baseline) and the recency level of step 7 → blended by the step-6 dials: par-centred → recent as qualification accrues; then an "established" treatment that holds wobbles of ≤3 points either way, credits a rise beyond 3 points on a smooth ramp scaled by an age-persistence fraction (≈0.92 at age 20, ≈0.38 at 29, 0 at 30+), and sheds a decline beyond 3 points toward the recent level scaled by an age-decline multiplier (≈0.85 at 25 → 0.55 at 37, credited back up for elders still producing far above replacement); finally the position-and-pick par level for that tenure is mixed in again at the pedigree weight — the par therefore enters the level twice, once inside the par-centred leg and once through this mix → the projected current level, points per game.
9. That level, for players ≥~2 years past draft → pulled toward a demonstrated-production target (the larger of a realized-forward-ceiling read off best-season-above-bar and career year, and the recency level) at a weight equal to years-since-draft × the share of ~14 games/year of opportunity actually taken; the pull works in both directions → an opportunity-tested level.
10. A mid-career season with zero games → charges an age-read penalty (a fitted level-point cost by age at the gap, capped at 20% of level), but only the shortfall not already charged by recency decay, and the charge dissolves with games played since return (weight (g+5.8)/(g²+g+5.8): full at return, ~0 after a season) → the final projected level fed to the forward models.

### D. The forward band — the core prediction

11. (Position, log effective pick, recency-decayed career games, tenure, a level feature, age) at the as-of year → a frozen five-quantile boosted-tree model, taught on resolved careers (debut ≤2021, one training row per player per as-of year, busts included at zero/low outcomes) whose target was the mean of the best three qualified season averages from that year forward; the level feature fed to the model is the par-centred level of step 8's first leg at training, but the refined level of steps 8–10 at live inference (the asymmetry of step 13) → five levels (10/30/50/70/90th percentile) of forward best-3 output, points per game; bust probability lives in the low quantiles, not in a separate multiplier.
12. The same features → a second frozen boosted-tree model taught to the 97th percentile of the same target → a sixth "ceiling" level, floored at the 90th; the ceiling's excess over the median is then compressed by age (retain 100% at 20 → 40% at 27+), with a demonstration-earned partial retention for players already producing well above replacement → the six-level band.
13. Both models are loaded at boot from pinned pickled artifacts and were taught on an older par-centred level feature while live inference feeds the refined level of steps 8–10 — a deliberate, permanent train/serve asymmetry (finding F3). The ceiling model has no fit path at all (a missing artifact halts the build). The band model, however, carries one silent exception: if its cached artifact file is absent, the build retrains it and rewrites the cache with no gate and no halt — outside that cold-cache fallback, refits are declared, gated, and not byte-reproducible.

### E. Per-year path and aggregation — where prediction and pricing entangle

14. Each band level L → a per-year output path over up to 18 future years: year-k level = L × fraction-of-peak at (age+k), read off a fixed table keyed to a per-position peak age (1.0 at peak, ~0.58 eight years before, 0.34 fourteen years after), floored at the current level up to peak and in year zero → the per-season expected-output path — the last object in the chain that is still denominated in points per game.
15. Each year of that path → immediately priced in the same loop: (level + a captaincy premium above ~105) netted against the replacement bar minus 3 through a soft-plus, ×21 (≈games per season), discounted 14% per year, reweighted by a form-conditioned certainty/fade factor (proven high-margin durable players credited in years 0–2; moderate-margin established players faded in years 5+; deep-pick over-optimism compressed), key-position ×1.05, and a young-elite runway bonus up to +25%; the six levels' priced sums are averaged at weights 0.18×5 + 0.10 (ceiling lightest) → the production price in value units. Boundary finding F2: the per-season expectation is never emitted; only this priced integral survives.
16. The current level alone → a parallel ≤3-year "demonstrated floor" loop (horizon shrinking with age toward 40) rolling the level forward decline-only along the same fixed fraction-of-peak table as step 14 — each year's level = current level × the ratio of the table's fraction at the future age to its fraction at the current age, capped at 1, per-position only through the position's peak age → a floor under the production price.
17. A curated injury register → an out-for-season player's year-zero level is cut by his lost-season fraction, his season counts as complete rather than grossed up to full-season pace, and a derived age-keyed haircut applies once at the return season — the return-season haircut carrying three gates: it applies only to proven players (4+ qualifying seasons; young or speculative names ship with no return haircut), only to register entries in the section marked for it, and only on the published-board evaluation path (the walk-forward teaching runs are availability-free) → availability enters expectation for register names only.

### F. Corrections applied to the price that are really expectation rules

18. Thin records → the price is pulled up toward the price of a synthetic at-par player of the same position/pick/tenure ("the pole"), at a weight = age fade (full ≤20, gone by ~26; zero for rucks) × tenure fade (1.0 year 1 → 0.05 by year 5) × an exposure gate, times a recovery factor (how close weighted recent output is to par, 0.54→1.0); the pole prices carry hand-set per-position re-level constants → pedigree carries the young, production takes over.
19. Proven output → the captain-free price is log-blended (strength 0.10 × a saturating evidence weight × an onset ramp) toward a target proportional to games-and-recency-weighted realized output above replacement, with a per-position conservation rescale; the captaincy increment is computed separately and added back unblended → output-proportionality correction.
20. Drafted players with <46 career games → price × (1 + 0.9 × R × (1 − games/46)²), where R is the measured historical one-year re-rating of that (position × log-pick × played/sat-out) cell at the first-anniversary mark, attrition and busts included, clipped ≥0, read from a frozen year-keyed leak-free table → the expected-rerating (youth) credit, full strength at zero evidence so it flows into the year-zero value.
21. Every player → price × a frozen per-position pick-monotonicity multiplier (built so a later pick at par never prices above an earlier one), faded away as evidence accrues → the pick-order guard.

### G. Special populations

22. A player with no record at all (board view) → his start value is read directly off a frozen year-zero surface: value = ruled-pick-curve anchor(pick) × lens(position, draft-age, pick), the lens being a bounded multiplier field (within [0.5, 2.0]) taught from measured career values (section H) with smooth Gaussian locality in log-pick (bandwidth 0.35) and draft age (1.5 years), confidence shrinkage n/(n+25) through (pos,age,pick)→(pos,pick)→(pos)→1.0, and a per-pick neutrality rescale so each pick-neighbourhood's anchor-weighted total is conserved over the actual roster composition; the lens bound is the only cap on this board value — the separate 1.4×-of-ladder ruck prior cap binds the raw year-zero estimate used on the backtest/guard path, the curve-miss fallback, and the superseded pre-lens fit, not the shipped lens value → the year-zero value (also the anchor for every penalty below).
23. A drafted player with zero qualifying seasons so far → value = (1−λ)·R·(year-zero value) + λ·(production price), where R is a sit-out retention surface (position class × log-pick × seasons sat, never increasing with depth, accruing concavely within a season, key-position retention floored at the general-position curve) and λ ramps 0→1 as games-at-pace approach the prorated 6-game bar → sitting costs value continuously; scoring output is information from game one.
24. Tenure ≥3–4 years with ≤1 qualifying season → price capped at (year-zero value × a declining fraction, base 25%) plus a graded release earned by current-season quality vs replacement; played-but-never-near-par careers of 5+ years are capped at year-zero value × a second declining fraction with a LARGER base (45%) but no graded release — a hard cap → staleness decay, all anchored to the year-zero value.
25. The season in progress → final value = w·(full-clock evaluation) + (1−w)·(evaluation with age/tenure clocks pinned one year back), w = 1 − s·(1−season-progress), s = 1 − current-season games/11 → on-pace players untouched; the clock advances only as evidence replaces it.
26. National draftees → value floored at (year-zero value × schedule: 45% in year 1 down to 5% from year 7); delisted or retired players → 2% of the year-zero value → the floor and the scrap value.
27. Future-year views (+1/+2) → the same machine re-asked with ages advanced but the form, evidence, tenure and pedigree clocks anchored at true-now, the age advance damped toward a sealed measured population transition rate per age (busts and exits included at full weight), and young (<46-game) values floored at now-value × (1−games/46)² → expectation of future standing, no separate growth term.

### H. Where the year-zero surface's teaching values come from (the loop)

28. Every player's history, truncated to each year 2003–2026 in turn, with clocks set to that year → the engine's own valuation function (this whole recipe) is called as-of each year → a season-by-season as-of value path per player, computed with the board-path year-zero surface live (the previous generation of the very artifact being refit).
29. A concluded career's path → its teaching value = the weighted mean of the as-of values where each season's weight = 1 − (normalized pedigree weight for that season): a season priced wholly on pedigree counts nothing, a fully self-evidenced season counts fully; a career with no season of ≥6 games teaches exactly 0 → measured career value, in value units.
30. A still-active career → teaching value = its evidence-weighted value so far × (mean full-career value ÷ mean same-point value) over concluded look-alike careers in the same (position × tenure) stratum with ≥20 members, busts' zero remainders included; a row with no adequate stratum (or nothing written yet) falls back to the engine's own year-zero prior for that player, and every fallback is counted and stamped into the artifact → the actuarial completion.
31. Teaching population → national picks 1–64 only, draft classes 2004–2022, two ruled #1-pick force-majeure exclusions with everyone behind them slid up one pick; the values are emitted as a pinned declared-input artifact carrying its provenance counts and its own stated +4.7% to +8.4% completion optimism → the lens basis.
32. The same structural values → the redesigned ruled pick curve: a Gaussian kernel over log-pick with bandwidth grown until effective n≥35, made non-increasing, the head scaled — not overwritten — to pick 1 = 3,000, strict descent enforced; adopted as a pinned artifact, never refit at build. Stated plainly: this ruled derivation is the redesign's method; the pick curve shipped on the board TODAY was derived by the older superseded method (a hard pick-1 overwrite with a time-kernel fit over a different, wider class window and population), and the redesigned curve replaces it when the redesign lands → the anchor of step 22.
33. THE DEPENDENCY, stated plainly (carried finding F1): the year-zero surface is taught from career values produced by a valuation function that leans on the year-zero estimate wherever a record is thin — every career's early seasons carry it through the pedigree term, through the year-zero anchor in every sit-out/staleness/floor rule, and directly through the counted fallback rows of step 30. On the pedigree term's weight: the 0.11 figure is the floor of the fade factor only — the high-evidence asymptote — not a lower bound on the pedigree weight itself, which is the product of a qualifying gate and that fade: approximately zero at zero evidence, peaking at roughly 0.35–0.37 near one qualifying season, and settling at about 0.11 for established players. The echo of the prior is therefore largest exactly where the teaching evidence is thinnest. The evidence weights of step 29 damp this echo but cannot remove it, and no number in the code bounds the residual share of prior inside a taught value outside the counted fallback.

## LIST 3 — VALUATION (expected output → the number on the board)

Currency used throughout: "board points" — the unit in which draft pick 1 = 3000. Scoring levels are in points per game (season-average scoring rate). The player population is every currently listed player (~805) plus this year's undebuted draftees; the pick population is national draft picks 1–64 plus one undifferentiated pool for every other entry route.

### A. Pricing one player from his expected future output

1. The prediction stage (the sibling list) hands over, per player per valuation year, a band of SIX plausible forward-peak scoring levels — the 10/30/50/70/90th percentiles of his forward best-3-season average plus a separately-modelled 97th-percentile upside tail (floored at the 90th) — in points per game; for every real player the tail node's excess over the median is then age-tapered inside the valuation wrapper (100% retained at age 20 falling to 40% at 27 and beyond, with up to 60% of the taper restored for demonstrated producers), so the band that actually prices is five quantiles plus an age/form-tapered ceiling; valuation starts from this band plus the player's demonstrated current level.
2. Each candidate peak level is expanded, inside the pricer, into a season-by-season expected output path: each future year's level = peak × the position's age-curve fraction at that future age (fraction table from −8 to +14 years around the position peak age), floored at the player's current level in year zero and every pre-peak year, stopping at age 38 or when the fraction falls below 0.42, at most 18 years out.
3. Each season's level gets a captaincy-scoring premium: zero at or below a 105 points-per-game bar, then a smooth logistic-integral credit whose marginal rate is the modelled probability of captaincy at that level (never above 1 point per point).
4. Each credited season is converted to an above-replacement margin: subtract that position's replacement level (per-position table, ~67–80 points per game) lowered by a uniform 3-point "acquirable replacement" allowance, then pass the margin through a smooth soft-hinge (scale 3 points) so below-replacement seasons contribute approximately nothing; on the current board a dual-eligible player's whole year-zero leg nets against the LOWER (more favourable) bar of his declared pair, taken at source with no proportional blend (an older season-remaining proportional-blend rule survives in the machinery but can fire only on backward as-of years, where a season-row bar can exceed the declared bar); later years net against his settled FUTURE position (probability-weighted across a declared dual stream, taking the more valuable bar on the alternate share).
5. The seasons are aggregated to one present value: each season's hinged margin × 21, discounted at the per-year futurity rate — 14% per year on the balanced board (a "win-now" lens uses 34%, a "future" lens 5%, and named postures 18%/10% exist as sketches) — summed over the horizon; then +5% for tall key-position players, and up to +25% extra for a young player near the positional elite ceiling (runway × eliteness product).
6. The raw present value is mapped to currency: value = scale × raw^γ, where γ (the value-curve concavity dial) is 1.0 — linear — in effect on this tree (config manifest, board runner, and code default all pin 1.0; 0.85 is the retired concave alternative still pinned by some old harness scripts); the scale is anchored so the 99th-percentile player on a frozen reference basis prices at 7000, then multiplied by one board factor = (3000 ÷ the head of an import-time curve fit that never ships) × the pick-curve derivation's published global head factor, so player currency and pick currency are scaled from the same measured head.
7. Each candidate's price is floored at a demonstrated-production floor: the player's current demonstrated level rolled forward only 1–3 years (horizon shrinks with age toward 40), through the same premium/netting/discount/currency arithmetic — a proven present can never be priced below what it demonstrably delivers over the near horizon.
8. The six floored candidate prices are averaged — 18% weight on each of the five percentiles, 10% on the upside tail — times a global distribution scale of exactly 1.0; this expected-value-over-outcomes is the production price, and is how upside convexity is paid and collapse risk charged (no separate option-value term).
9. For a PROVEN player (4+ qualifying seasons), every year-k term inside steps 2–5 is reweighted by a form-conditioned certainty factor: near years (k=0–2, fading out by 5) credited up to +17% × an above-replacement-margin ramp × 3-year durability × a not-declining gate (higher and top-tier-boosted for key forwards); far years (phasing in k=4→10) faded up to −60% for moderate-margin established players aged 26+; the year weight never falls below 0.05. Two boundary rules the proven label hides: the small measured deep-pick (41+) over-optimism compression in three positions applies only to sub-proven (thin-career) players and partially-proven key forwards — a fully proven player outside key forward never carries it; and a partially-proven key forward (2–3 qualifying seasons) with top-tier sustained demonstration earns the same near-year credit scaled by his qualifying-season count over 4 — the reweighting is not strictly proven-only.
10. A drafted player with under 46 career games gets an expectation credit: price × [1 + 0.9 × (the measured non-negative one-year re-rating of his class at his position and log-pick, blended between the sat and played sub-classes over his first 6 games) × (1 − games/46)²] — read from a frozen, leak-free table keyed by evaluation year; key forwards' cell intensity ×0.92; long-term-injured names have the games clock advanced by expected lost games.
11. A pedigree pole is then blended in: price := price + w × recovery × max(0, pole − price), where the pole is the engine's own price of a synthetic two-season at-par player at that position/pick/tenure × a fixed per-position re-level (0.93–1.19); recovery maps the player's games-and-recency-weighted level ÷ his par (0.54 at ≤30% of par rising to 1.0 at ≥82%); and w = a young-age fade (1 at age 20, 0 by 26, always 0 for rucks) × a tenure fade (1.00 in year 1 down to 0.05 by year 5) × a smooth exposure gate — so draft pedigree carries an undemonstrated youngster and dissolves with age, tenure and evidence.
12. An output-proportionality map (currently on, strength 0.10) nudges a real producing player's captain-free production price geometrically toward (his position's median captain-free price) × (his games-and-recency-weighted realized above-replacement margin ÷ the proven-population median margin), with weight = 0.10 × a saturating evidence factor × an onset ramp over the first 6 margin points; per-position totals are renormalized so the map conserves each position's aggregate; the captaincy increment is added back untouched.
13. A pick-order guard multiplies the result: per position, synthetic at-par players are priced at every pick, the prices forced non-increasing in pick, and the correction ratio (itself re-monotonized so a later pick never carries a bigger multiplier) is applied to real players faded toward 1 by exp(−effective qualifying seasons ÷ 1.1) — full strength at zero evidence, gone once evidence accrues.
14. Veteran read-through: an established veteran's record reaches his price through (i) the prediction band, which sits tightly around his evidence-weighted level, (ii) the demonstrated floor of step 7, which usually binds his lower band nodes, and (iii) the proven-player reweighting of step 9; his pedigree terms (steps 11 and 13) have faded to ~nothing — so his price is, in effect, the discounted above-replacement worth of his own demonstrated level declining along the position age curve, plus captaincy if elite, in board points.

### B. Population-specific caps, clocks and floors (applied in this order)

15. Real rucks: if the production price exceeds a production-derived ceiling — 0.80 × the engine's price of a standardized developing ruck at the ruck-median pick evaluated at the player's best era-adjusted level, × a smooth young-ruck headroom of up to +35% fading out by pick ~30 and age 25 — while still at or below his uncapped start value, it is capped there; a ruck with no qualified production is instead capped at 1.4 × his draft-slot value × the same headroom function (keyed to draft age here, to as-of age on the production ceiling). In addition, every real ruck's START value is unconditionally capped at 1.4 × his draft-slot value × the young-ruck headroom before it feeds any start-value consumer — the scrap, sit-out, staleness and floor rules, and the fitted start-value curve itself are all built on the capped figure.
16. Established key forwards (4+ qualifying seasons, age ≥24): any price above the engine's own price of his demonstrated level is split at the price of his SUSTAINED level (mean of top-2 recent high-games seasons, injury-season excluded with the window extended); the demonstrated-backed slice is retained at 70%, the slice beyond all demonstration at 55%.
17. A delisted player prices at 2% of his start value (scrap), rounded — no future keeper value.
18. A never-qualified sitter (zero qualifying seasons) prices at (1−λ) × retention × start value + λ × production price: retention comes from a class (ruck / tall / other) × log-pick × depth surface forced non-increasing in depth (a sitter never gains value by sitting; the tall surface is floored at the other surface), where depth = completed seasons + (season-progress)^1.5; λ rises isotonically with games-played-at-pace from 0 to 1 at the prorated 6-game qualification bar, so the price is continuous at graduation.
19. A stalled tenured player (≤1 qualifying season by tenure 3, or 4 for talls/rucks) is capped at start value × a fraction that declines with tenure (×1.6 for talls/rucks), graded back up by a measured evidence-release factor from any live part-season scoring; a played-but-never-near-par player (two more years of tenure, best level under 55% of par) is capped at start value × a second declining fraction.
20. The engine value is rounded to a whole number of board points.
21. Mid-season clock blend: for the in-progress season only, price = w × the full-clock price + (1−w) × the price recomputed with every age/tenure clock pinned one year back, where w = 1 − s × (1 − season progress) and s = max(0, 1 − current-season games/11); season progress is read weekly from one authoritative season-state file (0.83 at this tree's cut; fenced builds halt if it is unreadable); on-pace players are untouched by construction; result rounded.
22. Start-value floor: a still-listed national draftee's price is floored at start value × a fixed schedule of his years since draft (45% year 1, 35%, 28%, 21%, 13%, 9% year 6, then 5% flat); rounded.
23. Availability layer: for names on the curated long-term-injury register only, the year-zero level carries a lost-season haircut, the season is priced as complete at real games (no pace gross-up), and proven names get a derived return-season haircut applied at the return year; the register is the sole trigger — every non-register player is bit-identical with the layer on or off.

### C. From engine number to shipped board number

24. The displayed player value = round(engine value ÷ 1.0524) — one uniform historical re-base that puts players in the pick-1=3000 currency; ranking order is asserted preserved and anchor-pair ratios asserted within 0.2%; a parity gate independently reprices every player and halts the export if any board value differs from the engine's own number.
25. The other board views ask the SAME price function at other as-of years: the −1/−2 boards are true past re-values on truncated data; the +1/+2 boards run a forward lens whose form anchor stays at true-now (growth is credited only through the model's own curves — no lens-only growth term), and every forward age-advance is geometrically damped toward the form anchor by a sealed measured population transition rate per age (no damping at ages ≤22 — those values are fully form-anchored — rising to full damping at 34 and beyond, busts and exits included at full weight), with an under-46-game player's forward value floored at (1 − games/46)² × his now value; all divided by the same 1.0524 and rounded.
26. Owner overrides are display-only: a matched row gains an override block (factor, displayed value, provenance) while the engine value is untouched; a fenced build halts if any listed override fails to appear on the board.

### D. How the pick curve is derived (the ruled method) and how picks are priced

27. Input: one structural career-outcome value per national draftee picked 1–64 from the 2004–2022 classes (~1,200 rows; produced by the sibling list: busts at zero, active careers completed actuarially), each tagged with its recorded pick.
28. Fit: at each pick 1–64, take the Gaussian-kernel weighted MEAN of those values over log(pick), the bandwidth grown from 0.10 in 0.02 steps until the effective sample reaches 35 (cap 0.60); wherever more than 1% of the kernel's mass would fall outside picks 1–64 (picks 1–2 at the top, roughly 51–64 at the tail), replace the mean with a degree-1 local-linear fit at the same weights (floored at 0) to remove one-sided-window edge bias, leaving the interior bit-identical to the plain kernel.
29. Pool: apply weighted isotonic pooling (pool-adjacent-violators, non-increasing over picks, effective-sample weights) with NO pick-1 override — if raw pick 2 measures above raw pick 1 they pool into one head block at their weighted mean — then enforce strict descent (each pick at least 0.001 below the one before).
30. Denominate (the redesign's rule): compute ONE global factor s = 3000 ÷ the pooled head, and multiply the ENTIRE curve by s — pick 1 = 3000 becomes a true measured unit, never an overwrite; s and the pre-scale head are published inside the shipped artifact, and the player-side currency must scale by this same s (a loader halts on any incoherence among the pin, the head, and s, or on a pin that disagrees with the environment). Stated plainly: this ruled derivation is the redesign's method, and today only the published head-factor block and the player-side scaling follow it — the ladder entries shipped on the board TODAY were produced by the older superseded method (a hard pick-1 = 3,000 overwrite with a time-kernel fit over a different, wider class window) and were never rescaled by s; the redesigned curve replaces them when the redesign lands.
31. Publish: round each scaled value to an integer (minimum 1) and re-force strict descent on the integers; the result is the redesign's national ladder — exactly picks 1 through 64; pick 65 does not exist and no ladder the engine accepts may carry an entry past the single pool index. (The national ladder shipping today is the superseded-method artifact noted in step 30, not this pipeline's output; this pipeline's ladders are held for the redesign's landing.)
32. A draft pick's board price: pick n (1–64) = the ladder value from the shipped artifact, re-asserted at export to be strictly decreasing with pick 1 exactly 3000, shown at face value (picks are NOT divided by 1.0524 — the ladder is already in the numeraire); future-year picks appear on the +1/+2 views as 64 anonymous placeholder assets per year at exact face value, and the +1 held-pick posture view applies a single 10/15/5% (balanced/win-now/future) discount once.
33. Everything past pick 64 — national selections 65+, the entire rookie draft, the pre-season draft, and every pickless mechanism (supplemental, mid-season, international, post-draft signings) — enters THE POOL at one shared index carrying one position-blind level; order of selection inside the pool carries no value; differentiation comes only from position (the step-13 layer) and the entrant's own record.
34. The pool / mid-season / supplemental LEVELS (pending the owner's adoption word — the RULE, not a settled number): level = the plain mean of the same structural career values over the pool population in the class window, never-established entrants at exactly zero, active careers completed by a position × career-depth ratio built from concluded pool rows (any stratum under 20 rows falls back to the model start value, with the fallback share disclosed), × the same global factor s; the mid-season and supplemental levels are the same rule restricted to their entry streams with the completion table shared pool-wide (a disclosed choice), each published with a 95% interval and the instruction to read the interval, not the mean; the shipped ladder meanwhile carries a declared stop-point pool value to be re-measured on the converged fixed point.

### E. How a newly drafted player is priced (curve × lens)

35. A new draftee's start value = ladder value at his pick × a bounded position/age lens m(position, draft-age, pick) in [0.5, 2.0]; the lens is fitted from the structural career values (the model's own prior is barred as a fit target), with Gaussian locality in log-pick (bandwidth 0.35) and draft age (1.5 years), confidence-shrunk toward broader pools — (position, pick) then position then exactly 1 — by n/(n+25), then rescaled by a per-pick factor iterated to a fixed point with the bound so that, in every kernel neighbourhood, the ladder-weighted average lens over the population actually being priced is exactly 1: a position priced above the curve in a stretch is paid for by the other positions in that same stretch, never by inflating the class.
36. The lens surface is fitted once at a controlled bake, frozen keyed by a deterministic configuration signature (active ladder + roster composition + value-gate settings), byte-asserted to reproduce across machines, and LOADED at every board build; a build whose signature is not in the frozen set halts rather than silently refitting.
37. The draftee's board price then follows the standard machinery anchored on that start value: before any evidence his price IS the start value (retention 1 pre-season), decaying along the sit-out rule (step 18) until he qualifies. Before any evidence his LEVEL is carried by the forward band evaluated at his draft features, not by the pedigree-par mix: the applied par weight is the product of a qualifying gate and a fade — approximately zero at zero evidence, rising to a peak of roughly 0.37 near one qualifying season, then settling to a residual 0.11 for established players (only the fade factor alone runs from 1 down to 0.11) — while the young credit (step 10) runs at full strength; the same start value anchors his floor (step 22), his staleness caps (step 19), and his scrap value if delisted (step 17).

### F. The no-arbitrage principle

38. The design principle is ONE price = expected worth now, moving on news, never on schedule; the code ENFORCES: one valuation function answering every view year, an export parity gate (halt if any board value ≠ the engine's), the unconditional pick-1=3000 numeraire halt, strict ladder descent, two-sided player/pick scaling from one published head, and "a sitter never gains value by sitting" (isotonic retention); the code does NOT enforce: cross-view value conservation ("value converts, never vanishes") is a report-only diagnostic, and several terms reprice on the calendar rather than on news — the pedigree/tenure fades, the start-value floor schedule, and the staleness onsets all move with elapsed time, with the mid-season prorations (steps 18, 21, and the exposure-pace clock) existing precisely to keep the clock from repricing players ahead of games actually played.
