  THE ASYMMETRY THAT EXPLAINS THE BOARD FALLING. The surplus moves BOTH ways in almost equal
  numbers, but the charge is CONVEX in the surplus below the centre and FLAT above it — a row
  already producing above its bar pays nothing and cannot pay less, while a row below it pays
  more when its recent seasons are its worse ones. So a symmetric change in the surplus produces
  an ASYMMETRIC change in the board, and the board falls. MEASURED:
