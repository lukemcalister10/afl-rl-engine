The pick curve under Yardstick A and Yardstick B, side by side

First cut, measured this sitting — indicative, not the packet's filed derivation. ND cohorts 2006–2016, picks 1–64, n=700 entrants (145 still listed, all in their 10th season or later). Source: the ORDER 45 per-entrant matrix (store fb640ca0), pure JSON reads, no engine load.
What each curve is. A (paid) = the live entry law's day-0 price, averaged over the actual entrants at each pick — today's law applied to that cohort's real position mix. B (resolved) = what those same players' board value actually resolved to, discounted back to draft day on the board's own clock (flat 14%, grace-A: seasons 1 and 2 free for ≤19-year-old entrants — the identical convention A uses, verified in rl_model). Same 700 players in both curves; the position mix cancels.
B needs a resolution convention, and the convention is the decision. Three are shown:
 • B best-exit (headline) — the maximum of (board value in season s ÷ discount to day 0): the best trade-out you could ever have made.
 • B year-3 — board value at his third season, discounted; careers shorter than 3 seasons count zero.
 • B raw peak — undiscounted career-peak board value (no clock at all; upper bound).

Lines are 5-pick centred means (≈11 players per pick is noisy); faint dots are the raw per-pick means. Read the shape here, not the level: best-exit is a maximum over a noisy path and a board price embeds the player's then-remaining upside, so its level is generous by construction — total B is 2.00× total A under best-exit, 1.30× under the year-3 clock. Even the outright top-10 busts "resolve" at 800–1,100 under best-exit, because that's the price the board briefly quoted while the hope was alive.

2 · The shape comparison — each curve anchored to its own head (picks 1–5 = 100)

This is the chart the yardstick ruling actually turns on. Relative to what the head costs, B says the back half of the draft resolves fatter than A pays for it — but how much fatter depends entirely on the exit convention. Under best-exit, picks 12–30 land at 1.3–1.6× A's shape and 41–64 at ~1.5×. Under the year-3 clock, the belly (12–30) still runs ~1.2× hot but the deep tail (31–64) matches A almost exactly (0.98–1.00×) — the deep tail's excess is late-career optionality (Grundy peaked in season 6, Gawn in season 7, Kennedy in season 6), invisible at year 3 and only capturable if you can hold and time the exit.

Caveats, all of them. (1) This is the first cut the register (v860) said would be rebuilt properly inside act two's packet — treat it as the picture, not the filed number. (2) Censoring: 145 of 700 are still listed; every one is in at least his 10th season, so best-exit peaks are essentially resolved, but they are peaks-so-far. (3) Everything is in today's currency — A is today's entry law and vpath is the current engine run over each career; no era adjustment is claimed. (4) Pool entrants (pick 65+, rookie/pre-season/pickless routes) are excluded — the national curve prices 1–64 only. (5) The mean-B per pick is star-driven by design (that is B's Jensen point, not a defect): pick 64's 5.8× is a handful of names in n=10. (6) The resolution convention — best-exit vs fixed-year vs anything else — is your packet word; this page's job is to show that the word moves the tail's verdict.
