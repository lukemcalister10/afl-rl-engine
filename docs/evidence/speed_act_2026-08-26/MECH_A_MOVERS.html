Mechanism (a) — the movers, priced by the engine

READ-ONLY STUDY for the D5 mechanism word — indicative, not a candidate board. The engine's own o31_pi was patched in memory to the declared form (maturation ρ → ρ1/κ inside the pedigree multiplier only, κ = the already-ruled pick exponent), every one of the 804 rows re-priced through the real ev() (blend, D7 guard, ORDER 45 net all live), then the patch was removed and the live board reproduced byte-exact on all 804 rows. Gameless rows provably unmoved; cameo floors unchanged.
The study's verdict, stated before the table: the pure κ-mirror is the wrong shape for the diagnosed problem. Two variants were priced — SYM (the exact κ mirror; late picks also mature faster) and ONE-SIDED (high-capital rows only). Under SYM: 602 movers, net +12,909 board points (+1.8% — law 9 would demand this ride with recalibration). But the movement mass lands on young stars with 9–90 games (Jagga Smith +509, Harry Dean +433, Duursma +414, Reid +385 — all κ=0.50 top picks with real evidence), NOT on the thin-evidence class the decomposition diagnosed: rows with 1–8 games collect only ~7% of the total movement, and Harrison Oliver himself moves +12 (283 → 295). The reason is structural: κ was fitted for the sitter clock, and its mirror ρ1/κ bites hardest at mid-maturation (ρ ≈ 0.5–0.9), exactly where players already have evidence. The principle — capital conditions how fast pedigree hands over to evidence — survives; the strength and scoping need their own fitted curve, concentrated at thin evidence, which is what the combined build's prereg will put in front of you. This page is the honest cost of learning that from the engine instead of from an argument.
