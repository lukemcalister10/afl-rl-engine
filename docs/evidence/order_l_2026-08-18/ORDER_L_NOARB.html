What these tables are asking, in plain words

Every table asks one question: if you owned this group of players on draft day, and sold them a year later, would you have made money or lost it?

yr0 is always 1.000. It is the group's own entry value, the starting point. yr1 is what the same group is worth one year later, as a multiple of that starting point. yr2 through yr7 carry the same clock forward.

yr0→1 is the first-year appreciation. This is the number the verdict is about.

The 14% rail is the cost of carrying a player for a year: the roster spot, the list place, the opportunity. margin is how much room is left before that cost is beaten.

SELL-RED means the group loses value in year one. You could sell on draft day, buy the same players back a year later, and keep the difference. That is an arbitrage against the board and it should not exist.

BUY-RED means the group gains more than 14% in year one. You could buy on draft day, carry them, and still beat the cost. That is the same arbitrage the other way.

ok means the group lands between those two lines, which is where a fairly priced group should land.

The five bands are your bands: picks 1-10, 11-20, 21-30, 31-40, 41-64. The three above them (ALL 1-64, 1-20, 21-64) are the coarse view.

The pool arms are the entry routes that are not the national draft. RD is the rookie draft, MSD the mid-season draft, SSP supplemental selection, IRE the Irish experiment, UNR unrestricted free agents, and PDA/PDN/PDS the academy and father-son pathways.

The answer is the full built walk-forward matrix. Not the fast navigation calibrator. The W2 pre-registration names "the walk-forward per-entrant matrix" as its primary object and pins its md5 before the run. The scorer computes each class mark as R_cand = P1 / P0, where P1 is the sum of each player's vpath[0] — the engine's own walk-forward year-1 valuation — and P0 is the sum of entry prices. The gate G1 that the boards are scored on was registered as that scorer's mean_0515, and its recorded value for the landing candidate, 1.0421, is that scorer's output on the built matrix.

So what is 1.0324? It is the same kind of number on a different set of draft classes. The W2 scorer labels a class by its draft year and averages 2005-2015, which are the national drafts of 2005-2015. The two windows are shifted by one year at both ends.
