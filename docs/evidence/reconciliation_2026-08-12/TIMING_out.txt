F1. COMPOSITION CONTROL -- why the career-age axis looked like a huge timing effect
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  NATIONAL   n=1443   ever played 1250 ( 86.6%)   NEVER played  193 ( 13.4%)
  ALL POOL   n=1200   ever played  685 ( 57.1%)   NEVER played  515 ( 42.9%)
  RD         n= 691   ever played  379 ( 54.8%)   NEVER played  312 ( 45.2%)

  The career-age axis has no year for a man who never played, so he leaves it. RD loses 45.2%%
  of its rows that way and NATIONAL only 13.4%%. That is a COMPOSITION effect, not timing.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  For any arm at a given year:   ALL-IN  =  SURVIVORS' own-entry  x  SURVIVING ENTRY SHARE
  (exact, because the dead contribute 0 to the numerator and their entry stays in the denominator)

  arm @ calendar N=4             ALL-IN       SURV alive share      check
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
  NATIONAL (v0=anchor)           1.5547     1.7452     89.09%   1.554717
  RD @ signed anchor             1.3798     3.5047     39.37%   1.379793
  RD @ board v0                  0.5207     1.3328     39.07%   0.520725
  ALL POOL @ signed anchor       1.3381     3.2532     41.13%   1.338050
  ALL POOL @ board v0            0.4999     1.2009     41.63%   0.499940

  THE WHOLE STORY IN ONE LINE. At calendar N=4, on the SIGNED ANCHOR:
    NATIONAL  1.5547 = 1.7452 x 0.8909   (89.1% of national entry is still alive)
    RD        1.3798 = 3.5047 x 0.3937   (39.4% of rookie entry is still alive)
    pool survivors are marked 2.008x the national survivors' level, but only 0.442x as much of the
    pool's entry survives. The two nearly cancel: 2.008 x 0.442 = 0.887.
    THERE IS NO MARK SUPPRESSION OF LIVING POOL PLAYERS. There is a MORTALITY GAP, and the
    signed levels have already absorbed it by construction.
