THE MECHANISM, IN THREE NUMBERS
  1. the hybrid curve entering the smoother is STEEPLY CONVEX at the head:
       hybrid pick1 3191.2  pick2 2837.8  pick3 2732.7  (pick1 sits 9.3% above the mean of 1-3)
  2. a CENTERED MOVING AVERAGE IS A LOCAL-CONSTANT SMOOTHER. On a convex, steeply falling head
     it drags pick 1 DOWN, because every point it borrows -- even after edge replication -- is
     southern and worth less:
       pre-anchor head   ORDER 28 3191.1790  ->  CANDIDATE S 3028.7982   = -5.0884%
  3. the anchor pin pick1 = 3000 then DIVIDES THE WHOLE CURVE BY THAT DEPRESSED HEAD, so the
     head depression is converted one-for-one into a whole-curve INFLATION:
       anchor factor 0.940091 -> 0.990492  = +5.3612%   and the plain total moves +5.5109%

  THE TWO ARE THE SAME NUMBER. head move -5.0884%  <->  anchor-factor move +5.3612%
  The smoothing pass does NOT remove value. It INFLATES the curve, and it does so entirely
  through the anchor, not through the shape.

THIS IS A KNOWN DEFECT IN THIS ESTATE, WITH A RULED CURE
  o26b_loclin.py docstring, THE DEFECT (verbatim):
    "The shipped year-zero aggregator ... is a Gaussian kernel over log(pick) followed by a
     weighted MEAN -- a LOCAL-CONSTANT estimator. ... At pick 1 every borrowed point is southern
     and worth less, so the estimate is dragged DOWN (-7.1% measured). ... That is textbook
     one-sided boundary bias, and a local-constant estimator cannot fix it: it averages the
     slope instead of fitting it."
  ORDER 26B-C2 cured that by replacing the local-CONSTANT step with a local-LINEAR one.
  The 5-point moving average is ITSELF a local-constant smoother, so it RE-INTRODUCES at the
  head exactly the bias 26B-C2 was ruled in to remove: measured -7.1% then, -5.09% here.
