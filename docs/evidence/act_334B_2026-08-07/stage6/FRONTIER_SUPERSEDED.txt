#334 stage B / STAGE 6 — THE FRONTIER.  What bound this act, by how much, and what it costs.
================================================================================================

This file exists because two registered gates BIND on the upper half of the ladder. The seat did not
tune past them, did not re-pick a declared boundary after seeing which rung it struck, and does not
recommend a rung. What follows is the frontier as measured.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1 · THE BINDING CONSTRAINT — the monotonicity law against the zero-cell bounds
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The correction's power to be ZERO WHERE ALREADY PRICED comes entirely from its demonstrated-level
axis, Sz over z = log(e / entry_anchor): a player the engine has already re-rated upward reads a
high z and takes little or nothing. Measured, that axis is steep — the local residual runs +37.6%
at the bottom of the z range and −11.6% at the top.

But a steep DECREASING function of z is exactly what can invert prices: the shipped surface must
keep e*(1+delta) strictly increasing in e, or a strictly better career prices lower — the owner's
monotonicity law. Solving the two together (teach_log.txt, the joint block) the minimal declared
L-SMOOTH shrink of the Sz deviation is

    kappa = 0.693842      min d/dln(e)[e*(1+delta)] at rung 1.0 = +0.053337

and that shrink is what pushes the at-par cells back above their registered bounds at the top of
the ladder. The two laws are in genuine tension; this is the frontier, not a defect.

5 · THE CORNER AMPLIFICATION — the cost of the mandated decomposition, filed as a finding
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Addendum 1 fixed the kernel at TWO axes and made every other axis a declared shape gate. That makes
the shipped surface a MARGINAL (independence) product of factors that are in fact CORRELATED —
deep-ish picks tend also to carry low z and few games. The aggregate is protected by the single
declared conservation normaliser (Z = 0.715015, so |Z-1| = 0.285 IS the measured double-count), but
the CORNER is not:

    max |delta| the surface can produce at rung 1.0 = 1.6197  (nonKPP, pick 25, 6 games, z = -0.6)
    i.e. a +162% price move, against an aggregate the surface is calibrated to at +9.8%.

Realised on the current board at rung 1.0 the two largest movers are
    max-kondogiannis   355 -> 594   +67.3%   (pick 36, age 18, 8 career games)
    louis-emmett       619 -> 1024  +65.4%   (pick 27, age 18, 6 career games)
and at rung 0.25, +16.6% and +16.3%. Every mover at every rung moves UP; zero rows fall.

No registered gate caps an individual lift, so nothing here strikes a rung — but the number is
filed rather than left for a reader to find. A cap would be machinery the directive does not
authorise; an orthogonalised (rather than marginal) shape estimation would be the method fix and it
is a re-teach, not a tune, for whoever holds the next iteration budget.

6 · TWO ORDERING DEFECTS THIS BUILD FOUND IN ITSELF, ONE FIXED AND ONE DISCLOSED
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
(a) FIXED. The first cut ran the monotonicity law BEFORE the conservation normaliser, so the
    normaliser (which scales the base UP by 1/Z) re-broke the margin the law had just secured. The
    two are coupled and are now solved jointly. Caught by the probe suite, not by inspection.
(b) DISCLOSED, NOT FIXED. The pick-taper endpoint sweep that fixed the declared endpoints 34 -> 48
    was read BEFORE the same normaliser. Post-normalisation the picks 41-64 band moves +0.516pp at
    rung 1.0 against its 0.5pp bound — 0.016pp over. At every other rung it is met (0.129 / 0.258 /
    0.387pp). The endpoints were NOT re-picked afterwards: re-picking a declared boundary once you
    can see which rung it strikes is tuning, and rung 1.0 is struck on other grounds regardless.
