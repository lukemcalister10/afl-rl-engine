  the aggregate. A passing threshold that passes for the wrong reason is NOT a confirmation,
  and it is not reported as one.

  The binding-site diagnostic below shows what is actually happening, and it inverts the
  premise of the ladder order rather than merely failing to meet it.

  THE PREMISE OF THE LADDER ORDER IS INVERTED BY ITS OWN MEASUREMENT. The order framed H
  as "year-1-specific, no year-4 side effect expected". On the ND teaching population H
  reaches 0.2% of year-1 cells and 11.0% of year-4 cells — it touches year 4 about 66x
  MORE OFTEN than year 1, not less. H is not a year-1 dial. It is a years-3-to-5 dial.

  WHY, mechanistically — and this is the part that matters for the sitting. ITEM C's
  release is EVIDENCE-WEIGHTED (w = G*Q*gate, and the cap binds only where
  ceil < e <= V0_uncapped — a hot prior with no demonstrated growth). Year 1 is precisely
  the year in which a drafted player has almost no evidence, so e has not yet risen
  through the ceiling and there is nothing for H to release. By years 3-5 enough evidence
  has accumulated for the cap to bind, which is exactly where the binding shows up.

  AN EVIDENCE-WEIGHTED RELEASE CANNOT ACT IN THE YEAR BEFORE THE EVIDENCE EXISTS. That is
  a structural property of ITEM C, not a sizing problem, and NO VALUE OF H FIXES IT —
  raising H further multiplies a weight that is ~0 at year 1. The two year-1 movers in
  the whole 1197-entrant teaching population are both RUCKs (Kreuzer pick 1 +9.20%,
  Naitanui pick 2 +5.81%), reached through the ITEM E2 ruck cap at :2116 — the channel
  registered in the pre-registration as the CAVEAT, which turns out to be the only thing
  H reaches at year 1 at all.

  CONSEQUENCE FOR THE MENU, stated as a finding and not as a recommendation: H is not a
  candidate year-1 counterbalance. It is REPORTED, not re-sited — moving where ITEM C
  acts would be a wiring change well outside this act's ruled scope, and choosing to
  make it is the owner's call, not mine.

### THE NO-ARB CHECK — and the bound on lawful H
========================================================================================================
  Every rung charges the SAME flat 14.00%/yr, so as H raises yr1 the yr0->yr1 appreciation
  rises and its margin against that charge SHRINKS. Where it crosses zero bounds lawful H.
  The yr1->peak leg is annualised over its own span so the two legs are comparable.

  rung        H    yr0->yr1      margin   yr1->peak      margin   verdict
  --------------------------------------------------------------------------
  FULL     1.13      -0.26%      14.26%      11.34%       2.66%        ok
  H120     1.20      -0.25%      14.25%      11.34%       2.66%        ok
  H125     1.25      -0.24%      14.24%      11.34%       2.66%        ok
  H130     1.30      -0.24%      14.24%      11.34%       2.66%        ok

  THE BOUND. yr1 against H is close to linear over the measured rungs:
      yr1(H) ~= 0.9960 + 0.0013 * H     (fit on the four measured rungs)
  The yr0->yr1 leg becomes free money when yr1 exceeds 1.1400 (a 14.00% appreciation against
  the 14.00% charged to an 18-year-old). Solving the fit:

  NO CROSSING POINT IS REPORTED, AND NONE SHOULD BE. yr1 moved +0.0002 in total across the
  whole measured ladder (fitted slope 0.0013) — yr1 is
  effectively FLAT in H, so solving the fit for a crossing divides by ~0 and produces an
  arbitrarily large number (here H ~= 113.8). That is a numerical artefact of dividing by
  a null slope, NOT a bound, and printing it as one would be inventing a result. The
  honest statement is: on this instrument H never reaches the 14% charge at year 1 at any
  ceiling, because H does not move year 1 at all — see the binding diagnostic.

  THE ORDER ASKED WHERE THE MARGIN CROSSES, "AND THAT BOUNDS LAWFUL H". The measurement
  says the question does not arise: the yr0->yr1 margin is not the constraint on H,
  because H has no year-1 channel to shrink it through. The dial's admissible window
  [1.1024, 1.3327] remains the only binding constraint on H.
