### ORDER 4 — THE #336 DESIGN: EXPOSURE-WEIGHTED PAR  (owner word "let's look at your 336 design")
====================================================================================================================
  WHAT IT IS. The par surface is a PER-GAME benchmark, and it is the denominator of ITEM C's
  Q = sa/par where sa is the career GAMES-WEIGHTED average. Today every observation enters the fit
  with EQUAL weight, so a one-game debut counts as much as a twenty-game season and the fit answers
  "the average of season-averages". The dial weights each observation by its own exposure,
  min(games, 18). NOTHING IS DROPPED: every row of the #336 bust-inclusive sample stays in.

  IT CLEARED A KILL TEST BEFORE IT WAS WIRED. The suspicion was that exposure weighting is survivor
  bias by another door, because busts have little exposure. Three pre-registered criteria, all
  fixed in PREREG_ORDER4.md before the grid ran (full grid: XW_HONESTY.txt):
    A  it must not reproduce the survivors-only sample. 44 informative (tenure x band) cells:
       7 coincide, 37 DIVERGE. Pooled, tenure 1 coincides and tenures 2-6 all diverge.
    B  the sub-6-game rows must keep real weight. They keep 4.8% of pooled fit weight against
       19.7% of the row count (11.5% vs 36.7% at tenure 1) — down-weighted ~4x, NOT dropped.
    C  the added rows must still move the estimate. They pull it 3.05% below the survivors-basis
       at tenure 1, fading to 0.54% at tenure 6.
  AND THE FINDING THAT SETTLES IT: at tenure 1 in the LATE pick bands the exposure-weighted par
  lands BELOW the old survivors-only par (21-27: 54.37 vs 54.75 · 28-35: 55.13 vs 56.75 · 36-48:
  54.82 vs 56.20 · 49-99: 59.78 vs 59.91). In exactly the cells where late-pick bust risk
  concentrates it is HARSHER than the sample the owner ruled out. Survivor bias cannot do that.
