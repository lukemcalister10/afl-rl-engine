THE ORDERED _ruc_prior_cap CHECK -- the only surviving v0-chain law that touches this act
======================================================================================================================

    _ruc_prior_cap(p,v) = min(v, RUC_PRIOR_CAP * _cap_basis(p) * _ruc_head_v0(p))  for gfut=='RUCK'
    _cap_basis(p)       = pool_level(p) for a pool row  (_merged_recover.py:1209-1213)

  SO THE CAP IS EXACTLY PROPORTIONAL TO THE LEVEL THIS ACT CHANGES. Lower the level by lambda and
  the ceiling drops by the same lambda. Whether it BINDS depends on whether the capped quantity
  falls with it -- and _v0_uncapped = raw_ev(p, debutyr-1) * iso_eff(...) is a production-side
  object that does NOT read pool_level. So a level CUT tightens the cap against an unchanged v0.

  THE DIRECTION OF THE RISK, stated plainly: every pool pathway's RUCK cell has the HIGHEST
  lambda in its pathway (rucks are the best-delivering pool cell everywhere), so the ruck ceiling
  falls LESS than its pathway's other cells. Where a pathway is cut hard overall, the ruck cap is
  the least-tightened cell, not the most.

  WHAT THIS CHECK CANNOT SETTLE HERE, AND SO DOES NOT CLAIM TO: whether the cap actually BINDS on
  any given derived ruck v0 is a property of the machinery's OUTPUT, and the outputs only exist
  once the levels are wired and the engine re-run. That is adoption work by the order's own scope
  boundary. What is established here is the STRUCTURE -- the cap moves with the level, is not a
  fixed ceiling, and cannot silently clip a derived ruck without the level having moved first.
